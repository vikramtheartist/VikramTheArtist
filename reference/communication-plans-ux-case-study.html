04 · Research

Before evolving the experience, I went back to the evidence.

I used recent research, user interviews and customer feedback to understand where the experience created friction, what users valued, and how the information hierarchy could better support decision making.

01

User interviews

Understand workflows, expectations and pain points.

02

Customer feedback

Identify recurring friction and unmet needs.

03

Experience review

Evaluate how information was being presented and discovered.

FINDING 01

Important interpretation could be missed.

The Impact Summary was valuable, but attention could move to metrics before the higher-level story.

Design implication: Bring meaning earlier in the hierarchy.
FINDING 02

Users wanted stronger visualisation.

People needed easier ways to understand performance and relationships between signals.

Design implication: Use visualisation to explain, not decorate.
FINDING 03

Post-by-post views were harder to interpret.

Individual metrics didn't always provide enough context for understanding the broader communication.

Design implication: Start with the plan, then reveal the drivers.
FINDING 04

Analytics needed a clearer narrative.

The experience contained useful signals, but they weren't always presented in the order people needed them.

Design implication: Move from outcome → driver → explanation.

What shaped the direction

Research showed that the insights were valuable—but not always in the right order.

Before exploring layouts, I returned to recent research and customer feedback. Three signals changed the hierarchy.

What we learned from research

Key insights shaping the new experience

1

AI summaries were considered valuable

AI-generated insights help communicators quickly grasp the big picture and interpret what the data means.

2

Users prefer top-down storytelling

They want to start with overall outcomes before exploring contributors, channels, and deeper analysis.

3

Visual representations improve scanning

Charts, funnels, and visual summaries make patterns easier to understand and insights faster to find.

4

Communicators want outcomes before details

They first want to know if the campaign worked, then dive into what drove those results and why.

Design execution

Turning the hierarchy into an analytical journey.

With the structure in place, the page moves deliberately from interpretation and overall outcomes into evidence and deeper analysis. Scroll through the decisions that shaped each layer.

AIStart with interpretation

Answer “so what?” before showing the metrics.

Research showed that AI summaries could be valuable but easy to overlook. Moving the Impact Summary to the top gives communicators context before analysis begins. It works like an executive summary: orient first, then investigate.

HierarchyMeaning appears before measurement.
CognitionA concise narrative reduces the effort required to connect separate signals.
TrustAI attribution and feedback controls keep the interpretation transparent.
01What happened?

Build a fast, plan-level read of performance.

The first analytical layer stays at the plan level: reach, engagement, click-through rate and sentiment. The funnel makes engagement composition visual, while sentiment shows how audiences responded—before the user asks what drove it.

SequenceOutcomes establish a baseline before attribution.
ScanningLarge numbers, visual proportion and consistent card structure support rapid comparison.
BalanceBehavioral metrics and sentiment combine scale with audience response.
02Who or what drove it?

Connect the headline to its evidence.

Post-by-Post Breakdown links plan-level outcomes to the communications behind them. Communicators can identify which posts contributed most and compare reach, engagement, click-through and sentiment without leaving the analytics journey.

EvidenceEvery headline metric can be traced to its contributing content.
ContextRows preserve author, timing and message context alongside performance.
ContinuityComparison happens in place, avoiding a disruptive drill-down.
03Where, how and why?

Move from performance into explanation.

Focused lenses keep deeper analysis from becoming one endless page. Leader Impact reveals how contributors and channels changed performance over time. Comments and Themes then surface audience language, emerging topics and the deeper reasons behind the result.

Progressive depthComplexity is revealed only after the outcome and its drivers are understood.
LensesRelated questions are grouped instead of mixing every signal together.
Qualitative whyComments add human explanation to quantitative trends.

10 · Reflection

The hardest part wasn't designing the dashboard. It was deciding what story the data needed to tell.

The work reinforced a principle I carry into complex enterprise products: when the underlying system is complicated, the experience shouldn't expose all of that complexity at once. Good product design creates the right sequence — helping people understand what happened, what matters and what they can do next.
